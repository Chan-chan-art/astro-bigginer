## All about the Seattle Space Needle Tower

The Space Needle is a 605-foot (184 m) iconic observation tower in Seattle, Washington, built as the centerpiece for the 1962 World's Fair.

Key Facts and Design

Height: Stands at 605 feet (184 m) tall, with an outdoor observation deck located 520 feet (160 m) above the ground.

Speed: Elevators take visitors to the top in just 41 seconds traveling at 10 mph.

Durability: Engineered to withstand winds up to 200 mph (320 km/h) and earthquakes up to a 9.0 magnitude.

The Loupe: Features the world’s first and only revolving glass floor at the 500-foot level, which rotates 360 degrees every 45 minutes.

Views: Offers panoramic sights of the Seattle skyline, Mount Rainier, the Olympic and Cascade Mountains, and Elliott Bay.

Tickets and Visiting

Location: 400 Broad Street, Seattle, WA, inside the Seattle Center.

Cost: General admission tickets range from roughly $26 to $42.50, and combined packages with the neighboring Chihuly Garden and Glass museum are available.

Getting There: Easily accessible via the Seattle Center Monorail from downtown.
